# Agent instructions: The Trestleboard

If something I ask conflicts with this file, do what I asked and mention the conflict. If it would lose content or saved data, or cost money, check with me first.

## Project
- What this is: the Trestleboard, an app of 24 practices drawn mainly from Freemasonry, Kabbalah, Hermeticism, alchemy, and Christian mysticism, used as psychological tools, plus routines, a quiet timer, the Watch (a breathing companion meant to run in the background all day like a video: you pick one or more of four breathing patterns taken from the practices, or a mix, press Start, and it breathes with you set after set, with short rests between and no set end, over a blend of generated sounds (the same mix the top bar's sound settings control), showing where each pattern comes from while it runs; it has a large Pause button; it keeps running while you move to other pages, with a slim bar above the tabs (on wide windows, a card in the side pane) showing a small copy of the breathing shape and what to do now, and its breathing waits while a practice's tool is running or a routine or the timer is on screen, but not while a practice page is only being read), and *A Field Guide to the Western Mysteries* with a glossary. Mainly for my own use, but kept shareable.
- Platform: an offline-capable web app I use from my iPhone home screen and in a browser on my computers, hosted on GitHub Pages. I edit it on a Windows PC.
- Saved data is per device: each phone or browser keeps its own, and nothing syncs between them. There is no backup or restore (removed in Oct 2026). When opened as an installed app, it asks the browser to keep its saved data even when the device runs low on space (`navigator.storage.persist`, near the end of the script; asked at each launch until granted); the browser decides, with no prompt.
- Location: this folder is the whole site and the git repository, and it is public on the web. Never put secrets or anything private in it; save notes, reports, and drafts in the parent Trestleboard folder instead. If you can't write there, put them in your reply, never in this folder.
- Layout:
  - `index.html` is the whole app, in three parts: styles, then a JSON block (`id="book"`) with all the book text, then the script. It's about 430 KB, too big to read whole: search for a name and read around it. Edit it directly, and keep it that way: no build step, framework, or package manager, so GitHub Pages can serve the files as-is (the old build scripts are gone; don't look for them or recreate them).
  - Things a search won't tell you:
    - A practice's wording lives in two places: the book block's `entries` (by number) and `E` in the script (its situation label, tool, the tool's step labels and closing lines, and a copy of its name).
    - The breathing patterns `P` are shared by the practices and the Watch (see Don't break these); the Watch's four patterns `WB` point to them by number.
    - A practice tool's tone goes through `chime`, which moves the note into the music's chord of the moment (`chordTone`) while the soft music plays; a tool that plays tones adds the Tones button with `tonesButton`, and one with a Lights out view adds it with `lightsButton`. Use these rather than new sound code, so the Sound cues rules hold.
    - The Watch waits (`wHeld`) while a practice's tool holds `active`, unless the tool marks itself `quiet` (like the switch countdown); tools let go with `quit`. An open routine or the timer holds it too.
    - Every sound slider is built by `mixSlider`, so all copies of a slider move together; a slider's volume is its position squared, and the mix plays through a limiter. The Watch's Sound and screen exists twice (beside the Watch on wide windows, `.wside`; as a sheet on narrower ones), and the two copies keep their screen settings in step.
    - From 37.5rem wide the bottom tabs become a rail on the left, and from 52.5rem a side pane (`#side`) holds the Watch card (a quiet Start card while it's off), "On this page" on reading pages, and glossary definitions; both need the window at least 30rem tall, so a phone turned sideways keeps the one-column layout. The pane is hidden on the Watch page. The rail is CSS only; `wideMQ` (the pane's width and height test, which `paneOn` uses) must match the pane's media query.
    - `audioMode` sets the browser's audio session to "playback" while the mix plays (on an iPhone this is reported to keep it playing with the screen locked, which also pauses other apps' audio and ignores the silent switch) and back to "auto" when it stops, so practice tones alone mix with other audio and follow the silent switch. It also sets the Media Session title, state, and pause and play handlers. Neither the locked-screen playing nor lock-screen controls is confirmed on an iPhone yet. A pause from those controls sets `lockPaused`; on return, the visibility handler leaves the Watch paused and stops background sound.
    - A glossary entry's "Used in" list is built from the text (`glossUses`), with a few hand-set word patterns in `GLOSS_FIX`.
    - `HOURS` (the monastic hours) and `SECTIONS` (old section names) are unused on purpose, kept as possible reading material until the Watch's reading feature is settled; don't delete them as dead code.
    - Old saved keys stay on devices but aren't read: `tb:bvol`, `tb:voice`, `tb:rate`, `tb:vkind`, `tb:weather`, `tb:wday`, `tb:shadow`. `tb:log` (old before-and-after ratings) is no longer written, but the evening review (`dayReview`) still reads today's entries. The `ink` colors and the `literata` font were retired in Oct 2026; searching "ink" mostly finds the `--ink` CSS variable instead.
    - Lights out (`openLights`; the Watch's own is `wLightsOut`) was called the dim screen until Oct 2026; it isn't the Watch's Dim slider (`wDim`).
  - How it behaves (these are rules, not just descriptions): the Watch's setup starts from the defaults each time the app is opened. The routines are `ROUTINES` (each with a `name`, its practices by number in `ids`, its length in `min`, and a short description in `about`, written like any other app text); their keys `morning`, `reset`, `evening` stay as they are, and each routine's name says what it's for, not a time of day. The welcome screen opens each time the app loads, and again on return after a minute or more away, unless the Watch is running; its tour of the tabs shows only on the first open. The sheets close with Done, a tap outside, or a swipe down; Esc closes the Aa menu and Lights out. While Lights out is up, the page behind is `inert`, and closing it puts the keyboard back on the button that opened it. Text size is set on the `<html>` element and scales everything sized in rem, so size new things in rem. `#/practices` and `#/all` both open Practices; keep both. The top bar's sound sliders turn the sound on; the Watch's do so only while it runs; the slider volumes (`mixVol`) aren't saved. After a pause from the lock-screen controls, the Watch stays paused on return (Resume restarts it); a pause from those controls with the app on screen and only background sound playing stops that sound (`mixStop`). The 18 small practices were removed in Oct 2026; don't bring them back.
  - `fonts/` holds the two optional fonts (Atkinson Hyperlegible Next and Hanken Grotesk, SIL Open Font License, licenses beside them), trimmed to the characters the app uses. A few aren't in them and show in the device's font: → with Atkinson, ḥ, ✓ and ⇅ with either. So does any new character outside that set.
  - `sw.js` is the service worker (the background script that keeps a copy of the app on the phone for offline use).
  - `manifest.webmanifest` and `icons/` set the home-screen name and icon. `README.md` is the repository's front page on GitHub, not part of the app.
- Preview locally: serve the folder over localhost (on Windows, `py -m http.server 8000`, then open http://localhost:8000). Opening index.html directly turns off the offline features.
- Git: I commit and publish with GitHub Desktop myself. Don't commit, push, or publish.

## Don't break these
- Saved data: everything I save lives on each device in localStorage under `tb:` keys, read and written only through `store.get`/`store.set` (they add the prefix). Never rename, remove, or change the format of those keys. Saved items point to practices by number and to note fields by position: don't renumber practices or insert note fields in the middle; add new ones at the end. When an option is retired, a saved choice for it shows and applies as the default; leave the saved value as it is (don't rewrite or delete it).
- Offline updates: whenever you change any file the app loads (not README.md or this file), update `CACHE` in `sw.js` once per task, before your first preview if you can preview: add 1 to the number and use today's date (e.g. `trestleboard-v10-2026-10-19` → `trestleboard-v11-<today>`). Keep the `trestleboard-` start, because old copies are deleted by that name.
- `FILES` in `sw.js`: every path must exist and look like `icons/x.png` (no leading slash), apart from `./`, which stands for the front page; keep it. One bad path silently stops the phone from ever updating. Add every new file the app loads (a font, an image, a script) to `FILES`, so it's there the first time the app opens offline.
- Sound cues: the app doesn't speak. There is no built-in or device voice and no read-aloud; practices use tones only. Only one breath cue sounds at a time: the Watch's breathing and breath tones wait while a practice's tool is running. Keep practice tones in the music's chord while it plays. Ask before adding speech back.
- Shared breathing patterns: `P` is used by both the practices and the Watch. The Watch uses `P`'s timings and tone pitches. For a Watch-only change, give the Watch its own copy of that pattern outside `P` (beside `WB`) instead of editing `P`. Never edit `P` without telling me which practices it affects, and that the Watch's timing changes too.
- Book text: the `id="book"` block is one JSON line about 190,000 characters long. Change it only with exact find-and-replace (never by rewriting the block with a JSON tool), keep every `<\/` as written, and reload to confirm the app still opens. One stray quote or comma blanks the whole app.
- Offline use: the app loads nothing from the internet. Keep it that way: anything new (fonts, scripts, images) goes into the project. Links out to reading sources are fine.

## Interface and design
- Navigation (the bottom bar, moving between pages) should feel smooth and fluid. When working on it, short transitions like the existing ones and icons that match the tab icons are fine. Put any new animation inside the existing `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)` block, even though older animations are switched off in a separate `reduce` block instead (leave those as they are), and make scripted motion (such as smooth scrolling) respect the existing `reduce` variable.
- Pages should be calm: nothing overwhelming or distracting. No decorative animation, menus inside menus, or crowded layouts on reading and practice pages. Don't add new sections to the tab bar, or tab strips inside a page, without asking. Showing the existing tabs as a side rail on wide screens, or a second pane beside the main content, isn't adding tabs, as long as the page still feels calm.
- Don't add taglines, helper text, or extra labels to explain a confusing screen; fix the layout instead. Leave existing helper text as it is, unless it's shown with a control you're moving or removing (mentions in book text follow Scope instead): then it moves or goes with that control, and your summary says so. (The request to move or remove the control covers its helper text; this is the one exception to the Content rules' ask-first rule for helper text.)
- New drawings and icons should match the existing line style: outline only (`fill="none" stroke="currentColor"`), apart from small solid dots and the solid star that marks a pinned practice; rounded corners, `aria-hidden="true"`, and the same size and stroke width as their neighbours.
- Phone first, then responsive: build the narrow layout first (about 390 pixels wide, my iPhone), then let wider screens use the extra room. Base layout changes on the window's size (CSS media queries in `rem`, like the existing ones), and mouse-only touches such as hover highlights on `@media (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`. Never guess the device from the browser's name (user-agent sniffing): a desktop window can be phone-narrow, and iPads report themselves as Macs.
- When you work on layout, use the common width bands from Material Design's window size classes as a guide, not a to-do list: narrow (under about 600 pixels): one column, tabs at the bottom; medium (about 600 to 840): the tabs may move to a slim side rail; wide (over about 840): room for a rail and two panes side by side. Short windows (under about 480 pixels tall, like a phone turned sideways) keep the one-column phone arrangement, with no second pane. Moving the tab bar, or reshaping pages I didn't name, still needs a request that names them. The existing breakpoints (30, 36, and 48 rem, used by the Read tables and small layouts) stay as they are; don't move them to the new bands. The 600 and 840 bands (37.5 and 52.5 rem) are for new layout work.
- At every size: reading text stays about 50 to 75 characters per line (use extra width for things placed beside the text, not longer lines); tap targets stay at least 44 pixels (the existing `--tap`); controls stay clear of the notch and rounded corners (the existing `env(safe-area-inset-*)` padding); light and dark mode both work (dark mode follows the device unless Light or Dark is picked in the top bar's Aa menu); and nothing may make the page slide sideways.
- Tables on the Read pages already stack into cards on narrow screens; a new table there needs a `<thead>` row and the same `<div class="table"><div class="tablewrap">` wrapper the book's tables use.
- Every control needs a label VoiceOver (the iPhone screen reader) can announce.
- Every feature should feel natural on any device and window size: phone, tablet, or computer; touch or mouse. Keep the same features wherever the platform supports them; explain any unavoidable platform limitation before considering a workaround. Only layout and small touches (such as hover highlights or keyboard shortcuts) should otherwise differ. Every action must also have a visible control that can be used with a tap, a mouse, or the keyboard; no action may depend only on hover, a keyboard shortcut, or a touch gesture. This applies to what you build or change; it isn't a reason to revisit features I didn't name. The Watch in particular is meant to run in the background all day: everything timed on it (breathing, tones, bells, and anything new that changes on a schedule) runs on clock time (`Date.now()`), not counted ticks, with tones and bells set a few seconds ahead on the audio clock, because browsers slow timers in background tabs; keep it that way. Don't spend effort on iPhone-only workarounds; if I ask for one, explain what it would take and ask before building it.
